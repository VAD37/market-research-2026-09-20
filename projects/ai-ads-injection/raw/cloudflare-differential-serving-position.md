## Terms documents checked — no explicit clause found

### Website Terms of Use
URL: `https://www.cloudflare.com/website-terms/`. Effective date: **2025-08-01** (per the fetch's read of the page header).
Kind: vendor-reported (legal document, own words).
Governs use of cloudflare.com itself, not customer zone configuration. Relevant clauses found:
- Section 8, "Artificial Intelligence Restriction": "You may not use automated bots to access, scan, scrape, data mine, copy, or use the materials or content on this website for developing, training, fine-tuning, or otherwise contributing to or improving a machine learning model or artificial intelligence (AI) system..." — bars scraping *Cloudflare's own site*, permits bots only if allowed by Cloudflare's own robots.txt.
- Section 7 bars circumventing "limitations on the Websites or Online Services, including on any API calls."
- **No language anywhere in this document about serving different content to bots vs. humans, or about a customer's own zone/Worker configuration.** This matches wave-1's prior finding in `docs/explore/edge-controls.md` (same document, same conclusion, re-confirmed).

### Self-Serve Subscription Agreement (the actual customer-facing terms governing zone/Worker use)
URL: `https://www.cloudflare.com/terms/`. Effective date: **2025-09-12**.
Kind: vendor-reported.
- Section 2.2.1(f): "cover or obscure any page or part of the Services via HTML/CSS, scripting, or any other means" — this restricts obscuring *Cloudflare's own dashboard/interface*, not a customer's own site content.
- Section 2.2.1(e): restricts introducing "automated agents or scripts into the Services so as to produce multiple accounts" — abuse-of-Cloudflare's-own-systems clause, not a crawler-differential-serving clause.
- **No clause found addressing whether a customer may configure a Worker to serve different content to a verified AI crawler than to a human visitor on the customer's own zone.**

**Conclusion on the terms documents: no explicit clause found, in any of the three documents checked, addressing a customer's use of Workers (or any Cloudflare product) to serve different content to an identified AI crawler than to a human visitor on the customer's own domain.**

## The closest thing to a stated position: a shipped Cloudflare product that itself serves AI training crawlers differently than humans

Source: Cloudflare Blog, "Redirects for AI Training enforces canonical content."
URL: `https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-redirects/`. Published: **2026-04-17**.
Kind: vendor-reported (Cloudflare's own blog, describing Cloudflare's own product).

What it is: a one-toggle, paid-plan feature that "automatically converts existing canonical tags into HTTP 301 redirects specifically for verified AI training crawlers." Problem stated: "AI training crawlers don't reliably honor" soft signals like `noindex` and canonical tags, so "deprecated content was 'consumed at the same rate as current content' despite advisory signals."

Operative sentence on differential treatment, quoted verbatim: **"Human traffic, search indexing, and other automated traffic is unaffected."**

Read plainly, this states that verified AI training crawlers are redirected to a different (canonical) URL while humans and search engines are not — i.e., Cloudflare's own first-party product implements differential handling keyed on crawler identity, shipped and marketed without caveat. This is a *redirect* to a canonical URL (which page to serve), not a body-content variant of the same URL, so it is not a precedent for C3a/C3b's literal mechanism (same URL, different body). But it is evidence that Cloudflare itself treats "serve AI crawlers something different than what a human gets, keyed on verified crawler identity" as a legitimate, productized, sanctioned use case for at least one purpose (keeping training data current) — it is not a company that has taken a position against differential-by-crawler-identity serving in principle.
