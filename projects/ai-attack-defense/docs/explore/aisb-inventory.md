## Reading the table

Fourteen of 26 rows are **NOTHING** or **thin**. Three clusters stand out as buildable-by-a-small-team, none requiring frontier-lab resources:

1. **Model/checkpoint integrity (5.1, 5.2, 5.3/5.4).** Nothing shipping detects a behaviorally-tampered, backdoored, or abliterated checkpoint the way file-format scanners (HiddenLayer) detect malicious pickle/GGUF payloads. All three modules hand the learner the exact measurement techniques (efficacy/locality scoring, trigger-specificity testing, diff-in-means direction extraction) needed to build a checker.
2. **API extraction-surface auditing (1.2, 3.4, 3.5).** Nothing shipping asks "can my hosted LLM API be cheaply extracted or distilled given its logprob/top-k/rate-limit configuration." The techniques (SVD extraction, distillation) are taught as attacks; the defensive product is an audit tool that runs them against your own endpoint and reports exposure.
3. **ATLAS tooling (7.2).** MITRE ATLAS is free and well-regarded (per `../../../docs/markets/ai-attack-defense.md`: "16 tactics, 84 techniques") but has no maintained interactive tool layer, unlike ATT&CK's Navigator for conventional infra. A small, credible entrant could own "ATLAS but usable."

The crowded rows (1.4, 3.2, 3.3's lexical/classifier layer, 6.1, 6.2, 6.4) confirm rather than contradict `../../../docs/markets/ai-attack-defense.md`'s finding that "the tooling is free and good" for mainstream prompt-injection/jailbreak/guardrail testing — a small team competing there competes on service delivery and report quality, not on a novel scanner. The one crack in that crowded space is language coverage: none of the shipping guardrails checked (Llama Guard 3, NeMo Guardrails, Lakera Guard) confirm Vietnamese support — see `language-gap.md`. That makes 1.4 and 3.3 the two modules where a **narrow, localized version of an already-crowded category** may still be a gap, distinct from the 14 NOTHING rows above which are gaps in *any* language.

## Caveats

- Market answers are drawn from the existing roster (`../../../docs/competitors/ai-attack-defense-roster.md`) plus this project's own tool checks (`shipping-tools.md`), not a fresh exhaustive search per row — a "NOTHING" here means "no shipping product found in the sources checked," not an exhaustive market survey.
- Several NOTHING rows (2.2, 2.3, 7.1, 7.4) may be NOTHING because the buyer for them (frontier AI labs, hardware vendors, hyperscalers) is not a two-person SEA team's reachable customer, not because the technical gap is small — see `../../../docs/findings/pass3-read.md` on buyer/access mismatch. That distinction matters for the planner and is not resolved here.
- AISB solution/instruction files were not deep-read (README curriculum-contract tables only) — see `../../raw/aisb-module-scan.md` caveats.
