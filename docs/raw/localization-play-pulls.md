### Block 11 — Google China vs Baidu: exit was regulatory/censorship, not a localization loss

Source: Wikipedia, "Google China". URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_China. Channel: WebFetch. Type: trade-press/tertiary.

> Google's China search share: 36.2% (Aug 2009) → 1.7% (Nov 2013) → 3.8% (Jul 2020). Baidu held ~60% share vs Google's 31% at the time of Google's 2010 withdrawal announcement (Yahoo <10%).

> Stated reason for exit: in Jan 2010 Google announced it would no longer accept censorship requirements, "in response to a Chinese-originated hacking attack on them and other US tech companies, they were no longer willing to censor searches in China." COUNTER-EVIDENCE NOTE: this is the important asterisk on the "local team wins" pattern — Google's decline was triggered by a policy/regulatory decision Google itself made, not by Baidu out-localizing a fully-committed Google product. Distinguishes "local player wins on the merits" from "foreign incumbent self-exits over compliance."

### Block 18 — AI-era: does the clone playbook survive when the underlying model is a rented US API?

Source: MachineBrief. URL: https://www.machinebrief.com/news/death-of-ai-wrapper-startups-wont-survive-2026. Channel: WebFetch. Type: trade-press/commentary (single-author argument piece, not data-backed reporting — treat as opinion, not measured fact).

> Core claim: "When your competitive advantage is 'we wrote a better prompt,' you're one API update away from irrelevance."

> Stated replication timeline: "Year 1 (2023): Wrapper launches with features the base model lacks... Year 2 (2024): Foundation model adds those features natively... Year 3 (2025-2026): Foundation model replicates 90% of wrapper functionality at lower cost with larger user base, eliminating differentiation... Year 4: Wrapper exhausts resources or shuts down." "We're firmly in Year 3."

> Named casualties: AI writing assistants "Jasper (peaked at $80M ARR then stalled), Copy.ai, and Writer.com, whose core products were absorbed into ChatGPT's native capabilities." Meeting summarizers "Otter.ai, Fireflies, Grain faced similar pressure when Zoom, Teams, and Meet added native AI summaries."

> Core vulnerability as stated: "without proprietary models, data, or deep technical capabilities, wrappers possess only 'head starts' that inevitably expire as platforms replicate features."

THIS IS THE CRUX FINDING FOR THE AI-ERA QUESTION: the mechanism that let Taobao/Flipkart/Nubank win (payments, logistics, credit-risk infrastructure, regulatory standing — capital- and trust-intensive, slow for a foreign incumbent to replicate) is structurally different from "we prompted a foundation model well" — a US foundation-model vendor can ship the prompt-level feature in a product update, not a multi-year infrastructure build. The old clone playbook's moats (Alipay-style escrow, COD logistics, local credit underwriting) required things a wrapper does not have. A "local AI wrapper" that is only a prompt/UI layer on a rented US model inherits none of those historical moats — unless it adds a genuinely non-copyable layer (proprietary local data, regulatory standing/data residency, or a sales/distribution relationship the US vendor can't or won't build). This maps directly onto the Vietnam AI Law's foreign-provider requirements (Blocks 36-38) as one candidate for that non-copyable layer.

#### Block 28 — GoViet/Gojek Vietnam exit: NEGATIVE EVIDENCE #2 (a localized foreign player still lost)

Source: The Runway Ventures (VC newsletter). URL: https://www.therunway.ventures/p/gojek-vietnam. Channel: WebFetch. Type: analyst-derived.

> Timeline: GoViet launched August 2018; rebranded to Gojek Vietnam July 2020; ceased operations permanently September 16, 2024 — six years after launch.

> Stated reason for exit: "Vietnam was contributing less than 0.5% of GoTo's GTV," an easy cost-cutting target after GoTo's IPO and pressure to reach profitability by end of 2024.

> Stated failure mechanisms: (1) leadership instability — four CEOs in six years, departures tied to "change in business structure from parent company" decisions undermining local autonomy; (2) subsidies without stickiness — "$230M" accumulated losses funding "0% commission for drivers" and deep ride discounts, but "never built product-level switching costs to retain users once subsidies ended"; (3) execution delays — GoCar (4-wheel service) launched three years late (Aug 2021), an acquired e-wallet was "never deployed," letting Grab and Be lock up those segments; (4) By 2023 Gojek held 4th place in Vietnam, revenue "200 billion VND" vs Grab's "4,000+ billion VND."

THIS IS THE SHARPEST NEGATIVE-EVIDENCE CASE FOR THE LOCALIZATION QUESTION: GoViet/Gojek was run by a genuinely regional (Indonesian, not US) player with local branding and a local joint-venture structure — about as "localized" an entrant as Vietnam saw — and it still lost to Grab and Be. The stated cause was not "localization failed" but capital allocation from the parent (a 0.5%-of-GTV market got starved) plus missing product moat once subsidies stopped. This is direct evidence that being a local/regional team is necessary but not sufficient — execution speed and parent-company capital commitment mattered as much as local knowledge.
