## Critique of the MIT 95%-failure study

Source: https://www.marketingaiinstitute.com/blog/mit-study-ai-pilots — industry commentary/critique — fetched 2026-09-16

Three flaws alleged: (1) narrow success definition — the study "defined success as 'deployment beyond pilot phase with measurable KPIs' and an 'ROI impact measured six month post pilot,'" which the critique says overlooks other valuable outcomes; (2) ignored metrics — quoting analyst Roetzer: "If you're going to say something has zero return, how can you do that without acknowledging all the other ways that AI can benefit a business?" — efficiency, cost reduction, churn reduction, pipeline velocity not counted; (3) thin evidentiary base — the "zero return" finding rested on "52 interviews that the report itself admits are only 'directionally accurate based on individual interviews rather than official company reporting,'" and the 300-public-initiative analysis "lacked transparent methodology."

Conclusion of the critique: the MIT study gained traction "not through rigor but because it 'fit a convenient narrative'" for AI skeptics.

Label: secondary commentary, explicitly a critique — not a competing dataset, a methodology objection to the same study above.
