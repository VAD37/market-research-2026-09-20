### Block 12 — Resident Director requirement, legal detail (secondary/legal-services aggregation of Companies Act)

URL: search aggregation citing rafflescorporateservices.com's explainer of Companies Act 1967 s.145
Fetch date: 2026-09-16. Channel: browser.
Label: analyst-derived (law-firm/CSP marketing content restating statute — treat the statutory citation as reliable, the rest as CSP marketing framing).

> "Every Singapore-incorporated company must have at least one director who is 'ordinarily resident in Singapore' at all times. This requirement is outlined in Section 145 of the Companies Act 1967. The director must be a natural person, as corporate directors are not permitted in Singapore. The 'at all times' element is crucial; if the sole Singapore-resident director resigns or moves abroad, the company is in default unless a replacement takes office."
> "To qualify as a resident director, a person must be an Employment Pass (EP) holder, but only after obtaining a Letter of Consent (LOC) from MOM to act as a director of a company other than their EP-sponsoring employer. The person must also have a Singapore residential address, not just a UEN-linked office."
> "Nominee directors are often used by foreign business owners to fulfill the resident director obligation for statutory compliance purposes. They sign required documents and ensure regulatory obligations are met but do not participate in the company's daily operations or business decisions."

### Block 15 — Nominee director cost, risk, and 2025 regulatory tightening (secondary/legal-services content — treat cost figures as market-rate estimates, not government-set)

URL: https://expatmemo.com/nominee-director-singapore
Fetch date: 2026-09-16. Channel: WebFetch.
Label: analyst-derived (CSP-industry content).

> "Annual retainer: SGD 1,200 to SGD 6,000 per year (the post-June 2025 market rate through registered CSPs)" plus setup fees of SGD 500–2,000, indemnity drafting at SGD 200–800, and possible security deposits of SGD 1,000–5,000.
> "A nominee carries all director duties under Singapore law regardless of operational involvement. Courts have held that even silent nominees are accountable for oversight failures." The indemnity agreement "offers no shelter from ACRA enforcement, an IRAS audit, or criminal proceedings."
> Personal exposure: disqualification, fines, personal liability for company debts, enforcement action for tax evasion/fraud/money laundering.
> "CSP Act 2024" changes, effective 9 June 2025: nominees must be appointed through ACRA-registered Corporate Service Providers only; informal arrangements are illegal; CSPs must run "Fit and Proper" checks (criminal record, bankruptcy history) on nominees; appointment timelines extended to 2-4 weeks.
> "Critical trap: Appointing a nominee through an unregistered provider after June 2025 triggers regulatory action against your company, not just the provider."
> Banking red flag: "A nominee with no apparent business connection to the company is flagged during corporate account opening at major banks (DBS, OCBC, UOB), potentially slowing or blocking approval."

**Negative evidence for the "just hire a nominee director" shortcut**: it costs SGD 1,200-6,000/year on top of incorporation, carries real legal exposure to the nominee (which CSPs price into fees and vet founders for), takes 2-4 weeks to arrange post-2025 rule change, and can itself trigger extra scrutiny — and possibly blocked approval — when the founders then try to open the very corporate bank account the whole structure exists to get.
