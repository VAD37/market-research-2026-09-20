## 15. Robby Starbuck v. Google — Reason/Volokh detailed ruling

## 21. Germany — OLG Hamm chatbot liability ruling
Source: https://policy-insider.ai/who-blames-the-bot-the-olg-hamm-ruling-and-the-reality-of-ai-liability-in-professional-services/ ; https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/06/german-court-addresses-liability — legal-commentary press — fetched 2026-09-16 (WebSearch synthesis; primary Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch)

Ruling date: 2026-05-12. Court: Higher Regional Court of Hamm (OLG Hamm), Germany. Defendant: "a German medical company specializing in cosmetic surgeries," run by two physician-CEOs. Facts: the company's website chatbot, "asked, replied that the two managing directors were 'specialists in plastic and aesthetic surgery' and 'specialists in aesthetic medicine' — titles that the two doctors did not actually hold." Ruling: "The court classified the chatbot's erroneous responses as misleading and, therefore, an unfair commercial practice by the defendant under competition law," holding "the operator of an AI-powered program has sufficient influence over the system and initiates it." Key legal-implication line: "whoever uses a chatbot on their site bears the risk of hallucination themselves" — "a business is fully liable under unfair competition laws for the false or misleading statements generated by its AI chatbot — even if the company can prove it fed the system entirely correct data." **This is liability for a company's own first-party chatbot, not for what a third-party answer engine (Google/ChatGPT/Perplexity) says about a company — a different fact pattern from Starbuck/Wolf River/Hood.**
